The ten-second test: six ways to play it

This is the second screen of the new intro: a question, a clock, then three "but why?"s. Your screenshot is the quiet version, and it works, but it is a text page with a button. Each treatment below is the same test in a different skin, and each is playable end to end: press Start, answer, and get asked why.

Common to all six
  • The same script, the same three whys, the same end screens. Only the look and the feel change.
  • Nothing you type leaves the page, no model is used, and Skip and Escape still work from every one of them.
  • A reduced-motion version is calm: no shaking, no pulsing, the clock still counts.
A

She leans in

warmesteffort: low

Grandma is big on the left, listening. The clock is a ring that closes around her, and as you start she leans towards you. Her "but why?" is a speech bubble above your answer box.

try it
Why it worksIt turns a quiz into a conversation: you are answering a person, not a form. The ring makes the time feel physical.
CostNeeds the character art (we have it). Layout stacks on phones, with her above the question.
B

The exam paper

most memorableeffort: medium

A sheet of ruled paper on a dark desk. The question is written in a pen-like hand, your answer goes on the lines, and a red fuse shrinks along the top. At the end the paper gets stamped: "NOT QUITE" or "TOP MARKS", with a note in red pen.

try it
Why it worksEveryone has felt the dread of an exam sheet. The stamp is the shareable moment, and it matches "You think you know it" perfectly.
CostNeeds a handwriting-style font bundled (no web fonts fetched). The stamp text must never claim a real grade: here it only reflects whether you finished the three whys.
C

Say it out loud

closest to the producteffort: medium

One big microphone orb in the middle with the clock around it. The real product is a spoken conversation, so the test is too: you tap and talk. A "type instead" link keeps it usable anywhere. In this mock-up the orb only pretends to listen.

try it
Why it worksIt sells the actual experience in the first minute and teaches the gesture you use later.
CarefulSpeech recognition in a browser may send audio to a third party, which would break the privacy promise. A real build would use typing here, or the same local path as the rest of the app, never the browser's built-in service for a document.
D

A chat with Grandma

most familiareffort: low

A phone-style chat. She types the question, you reply, and she sends the next "but why?" after a little typing indicator. The countdown is a chip in the header that turns red.

try it
Why it worksNo explanation is needed. People know chat, and the typing dots make her feel present.
CostLeast visually dramatic. It looks like a messaging app, which could undersell the "voice" idea unless the next screen is clearly different.
E

Countdown theatre

loudesteffort: low

The clock is the scenery. A giant number fills the corner and ticks down, the background warms from calm to hot, and in the last three seconds the question trembles. The answer is a single line, because pressure makes you brief.

try it
Why it worksPure adrenaline in ten seconds. It makes the stakes obvious with no instruction.
CarefulCan feel stressful, and trembling is exactly what some people cannot tolerate. It must switch off under reduced motion, and the clock should be optional ("No clock").
F

Pick your question

most personaleffort: low

Instead of being handed a random question, you choose: three cards (everyday, science, work) or a box that says "Ask me about..." where you type what you are studying or presenting. The test then runs on your own subject, so the stakes feel real from the start.

try it
Why it worksChoice creates ownership, and a test on your own topic feels like it is about you, not a trick.
Honest limitThe "whys" for your own topic are generic ("But why is that?") because no model runs here. The real model can write sharper ones after the click.

What I would pick

  1. A as the base. It keeps Grandma alive from the first screen, so the test feels like a continuation, not a change of scene.
  2. Borrow B's stamp for the ending ("NOT QUITE" on a card that appears over her). It is the one bit that people will remember and share.
  3. Offer F's "Ask me about..." as a link under the question, so people with something real to present can test themselves on it.
  4. Keep C for the screen after this one, where the real microphone takes over.
