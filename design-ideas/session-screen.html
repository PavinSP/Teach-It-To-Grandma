The rehearsal screen: directions to choose from

These are mock-ups with made-up content, not the running app. Same moment in each: Lena has just said she is ready, and you are about to speak. Click the button to flip light and dark.

What feels dull today

  • About 700 px of information (title, description, source line, difficulty dots, three skill chips, the trap box) sit above the one thing you came to do: talk.
  • Everything is one flat tone, so nothing says "this is the important part". Lena is small and the microphone is far down the page.
  • The same layout serves a student teaching Grandma and a professional rehearsing a report, though they feel nothing alike.

Mixing the ideas

  • A + C: Lena on the stage, with the four named points under her and a pull-out cue card for the full text.
  • A2 for desktop, A3 on a phone: the side transcript where there is room, captions where there is not.
  • B for document mode, D for Grandma: the meeting room only appears for the professional door, and the student door keeps a friendlier look.
  • Whatever we pick, three things hold: a collapsed "lesson details" drawer, the local-only badge always visible, and one accent colour per character.
